bobi-stack FAQ
Short answers for your first weeks. Your Claude can answer any of these too; this is the version to keep.

The basics

What is bobi-stack?

A ready-made web project (Next.js, Supabase and TypeScript, already wired together), and a way of working with Claude that lasts: a briefing file, memory and checklists, so Claude picks up where you left off.

What is a box?

A Claude chat that owns one job: one project, or one kind of work. It has a folder and a log, so it outlives any single conversation.

What is CLAUDE.md?

The briefing every Claude session in your project reads first: what the project is, its rules, and how you like to work.

What is Claude's memory here?

Private notes Claude keeps for each project on your computer, not in the project: decisions, preferences, what's next.

Organising your work

What is the keeper?

One chat above your boxes (some call theirs MK, the master keeper). It remembers decisions that cross projects, passes messages between boxes, settles disagreements, and owns the structure.

Do I need a keeper?

Only once two or more projects need to share decisions. One project is its own box. Adding a keeper later takes about ten minutes.

What is a sub-keeper?

A manager for one area that runs several chats at the same time. It owns the area's shared tools and platform; it does not re-make its boxes' decisions.

When should I not open a sub-keeper?

When the area has only one or two boxes, when it would only pass messages along, or when it would re-check decisions its boxes already made. Start with boxes directly under the keeper.

What lives under the keeper?

Each project as its own box; boxes for jobs that cut across projects, such as security or marketing; and, only when earned, a sub-keeper with its own boxes.

How do boxes talk?

Through log files in the keeper's folder, not by chatting directly, so nothing depends on you carrying messages.

Who decides what?
  • The box that holds the context decides and does its own job.
  • The keeper decides structure. You decide plans, and anything involving money.
  • One box brings you each decision, never two.
  • A box never passes on your "yes" for another box to act on.

Day to day

How do I pick up next time?

Open Claude in the project's folder and say: "let's pick up where we left off."

Why does Claude disagree with me?

On purpose. It gives its reasons and doesn't fold just because you push back. You still decide.

What is a pre-mortem?

Before a big build: imagine it failed three months from now, ask why, and fix the cheap causes first.

Where do passwords and keys go?

In .env.local, which you fill in yourself and which is never uploaded. Never paste one into a chat; if you do, change it.

Something looks broken?

Tell Claude what you saw. It checks before saying something is fixed, and says so plainly when it isn't sure.

How do I change how all this works?

Ask. It's all plain files you can read: CLAUDE.md, the docs folder, and your keeper's logs.
